Lab Mission & Vision

Lab Mission

The mission of the Oladunni Lab is to advance the mathematical and computational foundations of pattern recognition, machine learning, and trustworthy artificial intelligence, with applications in cardiovascular health, epidemiology, and cryptography. We seek to discover fundamental principles governing how intelligent systems learn, represent, integrate, and interpret complex information. Through rigorous theoretical development, computational innovation, and experimental validation, we aim to establish generalizable scientific knowledge that enables reliable, interpretable, and equitable AI systems for biomedical applications.

We are committed to scientific excellence, original research, interdisciplinary collaboration, and the development of future researchers. Our mission extends beyond technological applications to advancing fundamental knowledge through high-impact publications, reproducible research, and meaningful contributions to computer science. We provide students with opportunities to engage in theoretical inquiry, mathematical modeling, algorithm development, and independent research, preparing them to become innovative, ethical, and intellectually independent scientists. As a research laboratory at a historically Black university, we are dedicated to strengthening research capacity, expanding opportunities for scientific discovery, and developing the next generation of leaders in computing and artificial intelligence.

Lab Overview

The Oladunni Lab at Morgan State University conducts foundational, theory-driven research in pattern recognition, machine learning, and trustworthy artificial intelligence, with applications in cardiovascular health, epidemiology, and cryptography. Our research seeks to uncover the mathematical and computational principles governing how information, including physiological information, is represented, preserved, integrated, and interpreted by intelligent systems. We develop and investigate nine complementary theoretical frameworks: Cardiac Stability Theory (CST), Complementary Feature Domains (CFD), Explainable-AI Trustworthiness (EAT), Physiologic Invariance Theory (PIT), Physiologic Energy Conservation Theory (PECT), Complementarity-Preserving Generative Theory (CPGT), Attractor Domain Theory (ADT), Attractor-Vascular Coupling Theory (AVCT), and Physiologic Trajectory Theory (PTT). These research directions address fundamental questions in representation learning, information theory, dynamical systems, physiological invariance, and computational trustworthiness, providing a theoretical foundation for advancing biomedical machine learning.

Our research translates these foundational principles into computational methods for cardiovascular monitoring, cuffless blood pressure estimation, physiological signal interpretation, and equitable wearable health technologies. By integrating mathematical modeling, deep learning, signal processing, and rigorous experimental validation, we investigate how physiological measurements can support reliable and interpretable predictions across individuals, sensing conditions, and demographic populations. The lab emphasizes reproducible research, theoretical analysis, and systematic evaluation, with the broader goal of establishing generalizable principles for trustworthy physiological intelligence. Through interdisciplinary collaboration and student mentorship, we aim to advance fundamental computer science research while developing computational technologies that address important challenges in health, epidemiology, and data security.

Current Research Projects

ORBIL-AI: Orthogonality-Regularized Biomedical Information-theoretic Learning

NIH R16 SuRE (National Library of Medicine), award 1R16LM015384-01 · PI: Dr. Timothy Oladunni · Students: Farouk Ganiyu Adewumi, Raymond Mbam, Daniel Owolabi, Obaloluwa Arulogun, Aditya Raj Poudel

Investigates how learning systems represent the information shared across multiple biomedical observations and the information distinct to each. Current student work examines whether models that combine time, frequency, and time–frequency ECG representations learn complementary or largely redundant information, using the PTB-XL and Mendeley ECG datasets and a research plan specified in advance. Project page →

Attractor-Based Analysis of Cardiovascular Signals

Frameworks: CST, ADT, AVCT · Student: Farouk Ganiyu Adewumi

Develops and tests dynamical-systems descriptions of PPG and ECG signals, including stability indices, the partition of attractor information into domains, and calibrated cuffless blood pressure estimation. Results to date are reported in 2026 preprints; see the Research page for the status of each.

Concept Drift and Stability in Multimodal Physiological Signals

Frameworks: PECT, PIT · Student: Farouk Ganiyu Adewumi

Studies when a model’s prediction should change under physiological variation, using energy-based drift criteria and explainability methods such as SHAP and LIME. Work in 2026 developed a CFD-guided framework combining ECG, PPG, and respiration (PECS), reported in a 2026 preprint.

Research Areas for Students

Cardiac Stability Theory (CST) & Multimodal Signal Analysis

Implement and test the proposed Cardiac Stability Index (CSI) on ECG and PPG signals using dynamical systems methods. Learn Lyapunov exponents, recurrence analysis, and entropy computation, and study where these measures are and are not reliable.

Skills: Signal processing, nonlinear dynamics, Python/PyTorch

Complementary Feature Domains (CFD) & Multimodal Fusion

Design deep learning architectures that preserve complementarity across time, frequency, and time-frequency domains. Explore fusion strategies (early, late, hybrid) that enhance robustness without redundancy.

Skills: Signal representations, neural architecture design, TensorFlow/PyTorch

Attractor Domain Theory (ADT) & Cuffless Blood Pressure (AVCT)

Study how cardiac attractor geometry relates to hemodynamic measures. Build and evaluate PPG-based cuffless blood pressure estimators using the proposed single-calibration AVCT approach, on clinical datasets and, as data become available, wearable recordings.

Skills: PPG processing, machine learning, mobile app integration, validation study design

Physiologic Trajectory Theory (PTT) & Longitudinal Monitoring

Develop Kalman state-space models for per-individual cardiac trajectory monitoring. Implement CUSUM drift detection to distinguish benign excursions from true physiologic changes in longitudinal data.

Skills: Kalman filtering, state-space modeling, time series analysis

Explainable AI & Trustworthiness (EAT)

Study interpretability methods for cardiac AI models, including saliency maps, attribution analysis, and uncertainty quantification. Test whether model explanations align with clinical reasoning and physiologic principles.

Skills: Model interpretability, visualization, clinical domain knowledge

Fairness, Equity & Demographic Validation

Evaluate PPG-based algorithms across Fitzpatrick skin types I–VI. Contribute to a planned longitudinal smartphone PPG study (IRB protocol submitted). Study demographic bias in wearable health monitoring.

Skills: Fairness metrics, bias detection, IRB protocols, diverse dataset collection

Lab Values & Working Philosophy

Rigor & Excellence

We state assumptions explicitly, plan analyses in advance, and hold our experimental design and publication ethics to a high standard, so that results withstand independent scrutiny.

Collaboration

We work together across disciplines, institutions, and perspectives. We value diverse viewpoints and collaborative problem-solving.

Reproducibility

We document data, code, and evaluation protocols, guard against leakage and other sources of inflated performance, and report limitations and negative results.

Real-World Impact

We study problems that matter for human health, and we are careful to distinguish research findings from validated clinical practice.

Transparency & Interpretability

We believe AI systems should be understandable and trustworthy. We develop interpretable methods and communicate findings clearly to diverse audiences.

Ethical Practice

We conduct research ethically, respect data privacy, acknowledge limitations, and consider broader societal implications of our work.

Obaloluwa Arulogun

ORBIL-AI: Deep Learning for ECG Interpretation

I’m Obaloluwa Arulogun, a senior at Morgan State University with a growing passion for artificial intelligence and what it can do for healthcare. AI advances every day, and I enjoy keeping up with where it’s headed, whether that means reading about the latest developments or trying out new tools for myself. When I’m not doing that, you can usually find me playing video games.

That curiosity led me to undergraduate research in Dr. Timothy Oladunni’s lab, where I work on ORBIL-AI, a project that uses deep learning to read electrocardiograms (ECGs). A single heartbeat signal can be viewed in several ways: over time, by frequency, or as a combination of both. My work asks whether models that combine these views truly learn something new from each one, or mostly learn the same thing several times over. To find out, I train and test models on two ECG datasets, Mendeley and PTB-XL, following a research plan set out in advance.

Farouk Ganiyu Adewumi

PhD Researcher: Multimodal Concept Drift Detection & Explainable AI for Cardiovascular Health

My name is Farouk Ganiyu Adewumi, and I'm a PhD researcher at Morgan State University, working in Dr. Timothy Oladunni's lab in the Department of Computer Science. My research applies machine learning to cardiovascular health, with a focus on multimodal physiological signals such as ECG, PPG, and respiration.

My work centers on stability and reliability in physiological modeling. This past summer, I contributed to the lab's CFD-guided PECS framework, which combines ECG, PPG, and respiration signals to detect concept drift and keep cardiovascular predictions trustworthy as signal conditions change.

Explainable AI is a core part of my research, not an add-on. I build interpretability into my work from the start, using methods such as SHAP and LIME to show which signals and features drive each prediction. This lets me test whether a model's reasoning is physiologically sound, not just whether its metrics look good. Working with Dr. Oladunni, I carry these ideas from literature review to experimental validation, and the lab's research direction shapes how I frame each paper.

Raymond Mbam

ORBIL-AI: Explainable Multimodal Deep Learning for ECG

Raymond Mbam is a PhD student in Advanced Computing and a researcher on ORBIL-AI. His research sits where artificial intelligence meets human-centered design: he wants AI systems in healthcare to be not only accurate but also understandable and trustworthy to the people who use them.

His path into AI started in design. Churn prediction estimates whether the user will continue to use a service or no longer use it. If a company can use this effectively, it can be used in various strategies for promotion. Particularly in the case of mobile games that use advertising-based revenue model as a business model. The number of users directly affects the profits, so it can be utilized more effectively. In this study, will examined the effectiveness of neural networks in churn prediction using real data from a mobile game that uses advertising-based revenue model. Marzieh Savadkoohi

This work investigates the balance pattern of human subjects through the force-plate measurements. Four different 60s quiet standing tests of eyes-open/firm surface, eyes-closed/firm surface, eyes open/foam, eyes-closed foam performed by 163 participants in three trials. Totally 12 force-plate files were recorded for each subject with the sampling rate of 100 Hz. The dimension of data for each individual subject is thus 72000 rows by 9 columns. Different Deep Learning Algorithms were used in this work to discriminate between human subjects and detect balance impairment. MLP, CNN and RNN were used as learning algorithms with classification accuracy of 99.8%, 99.6% and 96.9% respectively.

Vincent Tanoe

Bank Industry stock prediction using LSTM

Analyzing stock prices is very difficult due to the uncertainty and their dynamic nature. Investors use many statistical techniques such as moving average, Relative Strength Index (RSI) and others to make buying or selling decision on stocks investment. Stocks price are times series data which is a model that deals with a sequence of measurement of a same variable based on series of time. In this paper, we evaluate the stocks using an econometric approach using granger causality which is a methodology used in econometric to analyse short- or long-term dependency in binary or multivariate data. Another technique of analysis that is also beneficial for investors is the prediction. The second of econometric used in the study is the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA). Finally The study experiments a deep learning approach using the Long Short Term Memory(LSTM) which also cops with sequences of prediction using historical information and the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) which is a class of feedforward artificial network. In this research, we conduct a predictive analysis of historical stock price data of three banking industry stocks of New York Exchange (NYSE) which are JP Morgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo. We first evaluate their long-term relationship using their logarithm returns and follow by the prediction method. Based on the results obtained from the three methods meaning the LSTM, ARIMA and MLP, we conclude that LSTM outperforms ARIMA and MLP on the prediction.
