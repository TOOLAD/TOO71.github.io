Project Overview

ORBIL-AI investigates a fundamental problem in multimodal machine learning: redundant information across learned representations. When a model combines several views of the same signal, such as time-domain, frequency-domain, and time–frequency representations of an electrocardiogram (ECG), standard fusion methods optimize only the prediction task and assume that each stream will contribute something new. In practice, separate encoders can learn overlapping features of the same events, so that adding streams need not add information.

The project builds on the published Complementary Feature Domains for Optimal ECG Multimodal Deep Learning Performance (CFD-MED) work, published as “Rethinking Multimodality” (IEEE Access, 2025), and asks whether explicitly encouraging complementary, near-orthogonal representations improves predictive performance, probability calibration, interpretability, and generalization. It does so by adding two kinds of constraint to the learning objective: information-theoretic penalties on redundancy between streams (normalized mutual information and maximum canonical correlation) and a geometric penalty that pushes the latent representations toward orthogonality.

The questions are about representation learning and information theory; ECG classification is the setting in which they are tested. The Research Strategy notes that the principles are not specific to ECG; within this project they are studied with ECG data.

ORBIL-AI is a research project investigating fundamental scientific questions. It is not a clinical product, a validated technology, or a research center, and its methods have not been clinically validated.

Project Aims

The three aims below are summarized from the project’s Research Strategy. All describe planned work.

Aim 1

Develop and validate ORBIL-AI’s core methodology

Develop a multimodal ECG representation-learning framework with three specialized encoders: a 1D-CNN for the time-domain waveform, a Transformer for the frequency spectrum, and a 2D-CNN for time–frequency scalograms. Investigate a composite learning objective that adds three regularizers to the task loss: normalized mutual information, maximum canonical correlation, and latent-space orthogonality. Evaluate each component through systematic, hypothesis-driven ablation studies.

  • Ablations compare a baseline, each regularizer alone (MI, CCA, orthogonality), and the full model.
  • Regularizer weights tuned by a coarse grid followed by Bayesian optimization with early stopping.
  • Models, training scripts, and evaluation pipelines to be versioned and released publicly.
Aim 2

Evaluate ORBIL-AI’s robustness and trustworthiness

Evaluate generalization across publicly available ECG datasets, including device- and site-held-out testing. Assess predictive performance, probability calibration, explanation fidelity, and robustness to signal disturbances.

  • Calibration: Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL), and reliability diagrams.
  • Explanation fidelity: Saliency Alignment Index (SAI) and Attribution Matching Index (AMI), measured against expert-annotated ECG landmarks.
  • Stress tests from baseline wander to lead dropout, with documented failure modes compiled in a public “failure atlas.”
Aim 3

Capacity building and student pipeline at Morgan State University

Develop a structured undergraduate and master’s student research program with paid research positions, collaborative research teams (“pods”), reproducible machine-learning workflows, mentorship, and curriculum development, and support student participation in publications and conference presentations.

  • Paid fellowships for at least two students per year, with emphasis on multi-year retention.
  • Pods for data curation, model development and ablations, and evaluation.
  • Training in reproducible workflows (DVC, MLflow); weekly mentoring meetings and quarterly skills workshops.
  • A new course module, “Multimodal AI for Biomedical Signals,” developed with the School of Computer, Mathematical, and Natural Sciences.

Preliminary Evidence

The Research Strategy reports the following preliminary ECG classification results (macro-F1 on a held-out set). They come from the earlier CFD-MED work that motivated the project; they are not ORBIL-AI project outcomes.

ModelMacro-F1 (scale 0 to 1)
Single-stream 1D-CNN (time) 0.94
Two-stream fusion (time + time–frequency) 0.96
Three-stream fusion (time + frequency + time–frequency) 0.94

Adding a third stream did not improve on the two-stream model. The Research Strategy reports that this coincided with higher redundancy between streams and lower latent orthogonality, and that saliency maps from the two-stream model aligned more consistently with ECG landmarks. These descriptive observations motivate studying representation complementarity directly, rather than assuming that additional feature streams improve performance. They are not tests of statistical significance.

Scientific Significance

Redundancy and complementarity between representations are central questions in pattern recognition and representation learning, yet multimodal models are usually designed by heuristic combination of inputs. ORBIL-AI treats complementarity as an explicit training objective and evaluates its effects on calibration and explanation fidelity as well as accuracy, aiming at principles of how information is represented and integrated in learning systems rather than improvements to a single model or dataset.

Prior findings

What earlier work established

Peer-reviewed work preceding the award (IEEE Access, 2025) found that fusing time and time–frequency representations improved on a single-domain baseline, while adding a frequency-domain stream produced no further gain, consistent with redundancy.

Working hypotheses

What the project will test

That explicitly penalizing redundancy and encouraging near-orthogonal latent representations improves predictive performance, calibration, interpretability, and out-of-distribution generalization. These hypotheses have not yet been tested within the project.

Open questions

What remains unknown

How reliably redundancy can be estimated in high-dimensional learned representations, how the three regularizers interact, and when enforcing separation improves rather than limits what a model learns.
