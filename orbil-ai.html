Project Overview

Many biomedical problems provide several observations of the same underlying process: different signals, different sensors, or different mathematical representations of one signal. Machine-learning models that combine these observations are often assumed to benefit from every additional source. In practice, sources can carry largely overlapping information, so that adding them increases model size and computational cost without a corresponding gain in what the model learns.

ORBIL-AI studies this problem as a question about representation learning: how a learning system represents the information that is shared across observations, and the information that is distinct to each. The project examines orthogonality-regularized, information-theoretic learning, in which information-theoretic quantities describe how much information representations share, and orthogonality constraints encourage learned representations to separate shared from distinct information.

The scientific aim is to develop and test principles that explain when combining sources helps, and how combination affects a model’s performance and reliability. Biomedical signals, beginning with multimodal electrocardiogram (ECG) representations, provide the setting in which these principles are studied.

ORBIL-AI is a research project investigating fundamental scientific questions. It is not a clinical product, a validated technology, or a research center.

Project goals as publicly announced

As described in the Morgan State University announcement of the award (October 29, 2026), the project will:

  1. Study how learning systems separate information shared across multiple biomedical observations from the information that each source contributes uniquely.
  2. Develop and test principles that explain when combining sources helps, and how combination affects model performance and reliability.
  3. Move from characterizing complementarity to approaches that help learning systems learn complementary information more effectively.
  4. Provide opportunities for students to take part in fundamental research at the intersection of computer science and biomedical science.

Scientific Significance

Questions of redundancy, complementarity, and information preservation are central to pattern recognition and representation learning, yet multimodal models are usually designed by heuristic combination of inputs. ORBIL-AI addresses these questions directly, seeking principles that describe how biomedical information is represented and integrated in learning systems, rather than improvements to a single model or dataset.

Prior findings

What earlier work established

Peer-reviewed work preceding the award (IEEE Access, 2025) found that, in multimodal ECG classification, fusing time and time–frequency representations improved on a single-domain baseline, while adding a frequency-domain representation produced no further gain, consistent with redundancy. This motivated the Complementary Feature Domain (CFD) perspective on which the project builds.

Working hypotheses

What the project will test

That separating shared from distinct information in learned representations, through orthogonality constraints and information-theoretic criteria, may improve generalizability, interpretability, and calibration. These are hypotheses; they have not yet been tested within the project.

Open questions

What remains unknown

Whether patterns observed for ECG representations hold for other biomedical observations, how to measure complementarity reliably in high-dimensional learned representations, and under what conditions enforcing separation improves rather than limits what a model learns.
