Foundations of Pattern Recognition & Trustworthy Machine Learning

I study the mathematical and computational foundations of pattern recognition and machine learning: how learning systems extract, preserve, and integrate information from complex data. My work addresses representation learning, information theory, feature complementarity, and the robustness and interpretability of learned models. Cardiovascular health is the principal application domain, where these questions are studied with ECG and photoplethysmography (PPG) signals.

ORBIL-AI

Orthogonality-Regularized Biomedical Information-theoretic Learning

A four-year research project on how machine-learning systems represent the information that is shared across multiple biomedical observations and the information that is distinct to each, and on principles that explain when combining sources helps.

Research Focus

Foundations

Representation & Information

How learned representations capture, preserve, and combine information. Topics include feature complementarity and redundancy across signal domains, information-theoretic measures of multimodal fusion, and dynamical-systems descriptions of physiological signals.

Foundations

Trustworthy Machine Learning

When a model's prediction should and should not change. Topics include robustness to benign physiological variability, concept drift, the faithfulness of explanations, and evaluation practice that avoids inflated performance estimates.

Application

Cardiovascular Health

ECG and PPG analysis, cardiovascular stability assessment, cuffless blood pressure estimation, and performance across skin tones and demographic groups. Clinical and wearable-device validation are ongoing; see the Research page for the status of each line of work.

Research Overview

Research Vision

My research investigates fundamental questions in representation learning, information theory, dynamical systems, and trustworthy machine learning through nine distinct but related theoretical frameworks. Each framework addresses a specific scientific problem, from information complementarity and explanation reliability to physiological invariance, dynamical stability, and longitudinal monitoring. Together, these directions establish a research program connecting foundational computer science with biomedical information analysis.

Several frameworks are published in peer-reviewed venues; others are available as preprints or remain under investigation. The Research page gives the status of each.

Teaching Philosophy

My teaching philosophy emphasizes active learning, analytical reasoning, and practical problem-solving. I connect theoretical concepts with computational applications, encourage independent inquiry, and create learning environments that develop technical competence, intellectual confidence, and research curiosity.
