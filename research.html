Research

Theoretical frameworks for representation, robustness, and interpretability in machine learning, studied with cardiovascular signals

Research Program

The group’s research is organized around nine theoretical frameworks. They are related, sharing questions about how information is represented, preserved, and integrated by learning systems, but each is a distinct contribution with its own assumptions, claims, and evidence. They are not presented as a single hierarchy, and progress on one does not establish another.

Each entry below separates what has been reported, what is hypothesized, and what is still under investigation.

Representation, Complementarity & Trustworthy Learning

Complementary Feature Domain (CFD) Theory

Peer-reviewed

When do several representations of the same signal add information, rather than repeat it?

Core idea

Time-domain, frequency-domain, and time–frequency representations of an ECG can carry complementary or redundant information. CFD holds that the benefit of multimodal fusion depends on the complementarity of the fused domains, not on the number of domains combined.

Reported evidence

In a peer-reviewed study (IEEE Access, 2025), a model fusing time and time–frequency representations outperformed the time–frequency baseline across evaluation metrics under bootstrap and Bayesian analysis, while adding the frequency domain produced no further improvement. A targeted ablation supported the interpretation of representational redundancy.

Hypotheses & open questions

How far the complementarity pattern generalizes beyond the ECG datasets and architectures studied, and how complementarity can be measured information-theoretically and enforced during training. The latter is a focus of the NIH-funded ORBIL-AI project.

Outputs

  • Oladunni & Wong, “Rethinking Multimodality,” IEEE Access, 2025

Related directions: EAT, PECT, CPGT

Explainable-AI Trustworthiness (EAT)

Peer-reviewed basis

When is an explanation of a model’s prediction trustworthy?

Core idea

Explanations are treated as objects to be evaluated, not as illustrations. EAT sets out criteria for trustworthy ECG model explanations, including whether attributions are grounded in clinically meaningful signal segments and whether they remain stable under perturbation.

Reported evidence

A peer-reviewed comparison of intermediate and late fusion for multimodal ECG (IEEE Access, 2025) analyzes model explanations with saliency methods. The accompanying preprint describes testable criteria, including informational grounding to the ST–T segment quantified with adjusted and normalized mutual information, and perturbation tests.

Hypotheses & open questions

How the criteria transfer across architectures, datasets, and signal types, and how explanation quality relates to clinical judgment.

Outputs

  • Oladunni & Aneni, “Explainable Deep Neural Network for Multimodal ECG Signals: Intermediate Versus Late Fusion,” IEEE Access, 2025

Related directions: CFD, PIT

Physiologic Invariance Theory (PIT)

Preprint

Which changes to the input should leave a model’s prediction unchanged?

Core idea

Robustness is defined relative to physiologic drift: natural variation in rhythm, amplitude, and morphology that does not reflect a change in condition and should not change a prediction. PIT proposes metrics and training strategies that keep latent representations stable under such benign variation while remaining sensitive to pathological change.

Reported evidence

Formulated in a 2025 preprint (TechRxiv). The work has not yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Whether invariance learned on benchmark ECG data holds in clinical and wearable recording conditions, and how to separate benign variation from early pathological change.

Outputs

  • “When Should a Model NOT Change Its Mind? A Physiologic Perspective on Concept Drift in Multimodal ECG Deep Learning,” TechRxiv preprint, 2025

Related directions: PECT, EAT, PTT

Physiologic Energy Conservation Theory (PECT)

Preprint

How far may a model’s internal representation move when the signal’s energy changes?

Core idea

An energy-based criterion for concept drift. Under label-preserving variation, the magnitude of latent-representation drift should scale with the normalized change in signal energy; persistent violations of this proportionality indicate real concept drift. The criterion is operationalized as an energy-constrained regularizer (ECRL).

Reported evidence

A 2026 preprint evaluates the criterion on multimodal ECG across seven unimodal and hybrid models and reports reduced representation drift under physiologic perturbations. The work has not yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Extension beyond ECG is stated as future work. A CFD-guided extension combining ECG, PPG, and respiration for drift detection (PECS) has been explored by lab members.

Outputs

  • Oladunni, Ojeme, Maclin & Baidoo, “When Should a Model Change Its Mind? An Energy-Based Theory and Regularizer for Concept Drift in Electrocardiogram (ECG) Signals,” arXiv:2602.22294, 2026

Related directions: PIT, CFD, PTT

Complementarity-Preserving Generative Theory (CPGT)

Preprint

Can synthetic signals preserve the cross-domain structure of real ones?

Core idea

Generative models can collapse domain-specific information. CPGT imposes explicit complementarity constraints during generation, implemented in a quantum-inspired generative adversarial model (Q-CFD-GAN) for multimodal ECG synthesis.

Reported evidence

A 2026 preprint reports synthetic ECG that preserves time, frequency, and time–frequency structure and examines its use for data augmentation. The work has not yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Whether complementarity-preserving augmentation improves downstream robustness across datasets and tasks, and how to assess the fidelity of synthetic physiological signals.

Outputs

  • Oladunni, Ganiyu-Adewumi, Baidoo & Maclin, “Complementarity-Preserving Generative Theory for Multimodal ECG Synthesis: A Quantum-Inspired Approach,” arXiv:2603.26695, 2026

Related directions: CFD

Dynamical-Systems Analysis of Cardiovascular Signals

Cardiac Stability Theory (CST)

Preprint

Can cardiovascular stability be defined from the geometry of a reconstructed cardiac attractor?

Core idea

An axiomatic framework that defines cardiovascular health as the maintenance of moderate dynamical complexity on a bounded, quasi-periodic cardiac attractor. CST defines a Cardiac Stability Index (CSI) from three geometric properties: the largest Lyapunov exponent, recurrence determinism, and signal entropy.

Reported evidence

Formulated in a 2026 preprint. A related 2026 preprint evaluates a stabilized index (SCSI) on 176,742 segments from four PPG datasets, identifies three evaluation artifacts that inflated a heuristic AUC from 0.573 to 0.752, and reports a pooled AUC of 0.757 (95% CI 0.686–0.828) and NPV of 0.966 for tachypnea screening on 18 held-out records; per-record AUC was 0.497 ± 0.207. Neither preprint has yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Whether the index tracks clinically meaningful change within individuals, given the variability in per-record performance. The index is used in a lab prototype application (HeartSpan Score) for research purposes.

Outputs

  • Oladunni & Ganiyu-Adewumi, “Cardiac Stability Theory,” arXiv:2604.23876, 2026
  • Oladunni & Ganiyu-Adewumi, “A Nonlinear Complexity Index for Wearable PPG Cardiovascular Stability,” arXiv:2605.18802, 2026

Related directions: ADT, AVCT, PTT

Attractor Domain Theory (ADT)

Preprint

Which properties of a reconstructed attractor carry which cardiovascular information, and why?

Core idea

ADT proposes that the information in a reconstructed cardiac attractor partitions into three mutually non-redundant domains: a Geometry Domain (delay embedding; artifact rejection), an Ergodic Domain (asymptotic statistical invariants; stability estimation), and a Variational Domain (finite-time Lyapunov exponent field; hemodynamic inference). A Domain Sufficiency Theorem states that the three domains are necessary and sufficient.

Reported evidence

A 2026 preprint reports Geometry Domain validation through the SCSI index on 176,742 PPG segments from four datasets (AUC 0.757, 95% CI 0.686–0.828; NPV 0.966 for tachypnea screening on held-out records, after correcting three evaluation artifacts). The work has not yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Empirical validation of the Ergodic and Variational domains, and independent review of the Domain Sufficiency Theorem.

Outputs

  • Oladunni & Ganiyu-Adewumi, “Attractor Domain Theory: A Mathematical Framework for Cardiovascular Attractor Analysis with Wearable Photoplethysmography (PPG) Validation,” arXiv:2606.22039, 2026

Related directions: CST, AVCT

Attractor-Vascular Coupling Theory (AVCT)

Preprint

Can the attractor geometry of a PPG signal support calibrated, cuffless blood pressure estimation?

Core idea

AVCT formalizes a coupling between vascular mechanics and the geometry of the cardiac attractor, operationalized with Takens delay embedding and attractor morphology features. The preprint gives two theorems, a proposition, and a corollary.

Reported evidence

A 2026 preprint reports leave-one-subject-out evaluation on 46 subjects from retrospective intensive-care (BIDMC, n = 9) and surgical (VitalDB, n = 37) recordings, comprising 29,684 windows, with single-point calibration: systolic MAE 2.05 mmHg and diastolic MAE 1.67 mmHg, within the AAMI/IEEE mean-error criterion. A PPG-only ablation using nine attractor features suitable for smartphone computation performed within 0.05 mmHg of the ECG + PPG model. The work has not yet completed peer review.

Hypotheses & open questions

Performance on PPG acquired by smartphone cameras, in ambulatory settings, and across skin tones has not yet been established. The method is a research result, not a validated medical device.

Outputs

  • Oladunni & Ganiyu-Adewumi, “Attractor-Vascular Coupling Theory,” arXiv:2605.10871, 2026
  • Invention disclosure submitted to the Morgan State University Office of Technology Transfer (2026).

Related directions: ADT, CST

Physiologic Trajectory Theory (PTT)

Ongoing investigation

What constitutes a valid physiologic trajectory, as opposed to a meaningful departure from one?

Core idea

PTT proposes per-individual admissible corridors for a stability index, estimated with Kalman state-space models, in place of comparison against population norms. Departures are classified with CUSUM sequential detection as Stable, Benign Excursion, or True Drift, guided by a proposed Cardiac Asymmetry Principle.

Reported evidence

No results have been reported yet. PTT is a working framework.

Hypotheses & open questions

Evaluation is planned on longitudinal smartphone PPG data under an IRB protocol that has been submitted (target enrollment 500 participants across Fitzpatrick skin types I–VI). The dataset is intended for public release, subject to IRB approval and participant consent.

Outputs

  • Manuscript in preparation.

Related directions: CST, PECT, PIT

Application Areas

Cardiovascular Signal Analysis

ECG and PPG analysis for cardiac abnormality detection, including earlier work on suspected hypertrophic cardiomyopathy from single-lead wearable ECG (IEEE Sensors Letters, 2021) and multimodal ECG classification (IEEE Access, 2025). Longitudinal monitoring (PTT) is under investigation.

Cuffless Blood Pressure Estimation

Calibrated blood pressure estimation from attractor features of PPG (AVCT). Results to date come from retrospective intensive-care and surgical recordings and are reported in a preprint; smartphone and ambulatory validation are future work.

Equity Across Skin Tones

Optical PPG signals can vary with skin pigmentation. A longitudinal smartphone PPG study spanning Fitzpatrick skin types I–VI has been submitted for IRB review, to support evaluation of performance across demographic groups.
